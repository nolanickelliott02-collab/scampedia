I Think I've Already Been Scammed

If you sent money in the last few hours, stop reading and contact whoever you sent it through immediately — tell them it was fraud and ask if it can be stopped. Everything below tells you exactly who that is and what to say, for however you paid.

Where Did This Come From?

Before you look at anything else, notice one thing — did you go looking for this, or did it arrive on its own? Something you didn't ask for deserves a different level of attention than something you went and found yourself.

 Free

Reverse Image Search

Reverse image search shows you every other place a photo has shown up online — which reveals whether it's an old stock photo, a picture of someone else being used without their knowledge, or something that's been circulating for years before it landed in your message. It's free, takes under a minute, and works from your phone or a computer.

 Free

What Is It Actually Asking You to Do?

Four things show up in almost every scam request — urgency, secrecy, an unusual payment method, and pressure to keep it from people who'd normally help — and they're not four separate warning signs, they're one system working together. A real photo or a familiar voice attached to a request shaped like that is still a scam.

 Free

Content Credentials and C2PA

Some photos and videos now carry a real, checkable record of where they came from, called Content Credentials — but most images never had one, and a photo missing one is not more suspicious than any other ordinary photo. Presence can tell you something real; absence tells you nothing at all.

 Free

Why "Just Look at the Hands" Stopped Working

Counting fingers or looking for garbled text used to be a real way to catch an AI-generated image — it doesn't work reliably anymore. Every visual flaw that became well-known enough to teach is exactly the flaw a model's next version gets built to fix.

 Free

What to Do When You're Not Sure

When you can't tell whether something's real, the fix isn't to look closer — it's to reach out a different way than the message reached you. Call the number already saved in your phone, not the one you were just given.

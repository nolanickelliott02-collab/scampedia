Prize-Linked Social Security Direct Deposit Redirect Scam

Added Nov 8, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· 🏛️ Government-Verified· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: Social Security Administration Office of the Inspector General (SSA OIG)

Overview

Scammers tell victims they've won a prize or contest and, to "claim" it, ask them to switch their Social Security benefit direct deposit to a new account or prepaid debit card — actually hijacking the victim's monthly benefit payments.

How It Works

A caller tells the victim they've won a prize or contest, and that to receive it they need to switch their Social Security direct deposit from their own bank account to a different account, such as a prepaid debit card. To make this "switch," the scammer asks the victim for personal and banking information, which is then used to redirect the victim's actual Social Security benefit payments to an account the scammer controls. Victims discover they no longer receive their own Social Security payments once the deposit has been redirected, and if they try to reverse the change, the scammer may call back and become hostile.

Red Flags

  • An unsolicited call, text, or notification claiming you've won a prize or contest tied to Social Security
  • Being asked to change your Social Security direct deposit information to a prepaid debit card or unfamiliar account in order to "claim" a prize
  • Requests for personal and banking information to process a supposed prize deposit
  • Pressure or hostility from the caller if you try to undo a direct deposit change
  • Any legitimate-sounding claim that a contest or prize requires redirecting a federal benefit payment

Real Examples

  • Victims have been told they'd won a contest and, to receive the prize, were asked to switch their Social Security direct deposit from their bank to a prepaid debit card, after which they lost access to their own benefit payments
  • Scammers who redirected a victim's direct deposit have sometimes called back and grown hostile when the victim tried to undo the change

Where It Spreads

Phone CallsText Messages

How to Protect Yourself

  • Never change your direct deposit information because of an unsolicited call, text, or prize notification
  • Review and change your Social Security direct deposit information yourself, safely, through your personal my Social Security account at ssa.gov/myaccount
  • If you think your direct deposit has already been changed without your permission, contact SSA immediately at 1-800-772-1213
  • No legitimate contest or prize ever requires you to redirect a federal benefit payment to claim it
  • Report the scam to ssa.gov/scam or to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov

Car Wrap Advertising Scam

Added Sep 18, 2026· 🧠 Discovered by VerifyGuard AI Brain· Source: Better Business Bureau, "BBB Scam Alert: Car wrap scams hook job seekers with promise of easy money," February 19, 2021

Overview

Scammers offer people hundreds of dollars a week to drive around with their car wrapped in a major brand's advertising, then send a fake cashier's check that leaves victims owing the bank money after they wire funds to a "vendor."

How It Works

A victim sees an ad or gets a text claiming a major brand will pay them hundreds of dollars a week to wrap their car in company advertising. After filling out an application, a "boss" contacts them by text with instructions and mails a cashier's check meant to cover the cost of installing the wrap. The victim is told to deposit the check and use part of it to pay a specific "local vendor" via Venmo or Cash App who will supposedly install the wrap. Because the check initially appears to clear, the victim sends real money to the scammer-controlled vendor before the bank discovers the check is counterfeit, leaving the victim responsible for repaying the full fake amount.

Red Flags

  • Unsolicited offer to pay hundreds of dollars a week just for driving with an ad wrap on your car
  • You're sent a check and told to deposit it, then immediately send part of the money elsewhere via Venmo or Cash App
  • The employer contacts you only through text message and never in person or via a verifiable company channel
  • You never met the "vendor" and were told exactly which one to pay
  • The job requires no interview and pays out before any actual work (driving/advertising) has occurred

Real Examples

  • In BBB's own reporting, one victim described the scam directly: told to deposit the check and pay a vendor through Venmo once it cleared, they sent $3,500 — then waited two days for someone to come wrap their car. No one ever came, and the vendor kept making excuses when contacted.

Where It Spreads

Text MessagesOnline Job Ads

How to Protect Yourself

  • Never spend money from a deposited check or send any of it elsewhere before the check has fully cleared, which can take weeks even if the funds appear available sooner
  • Know that banks are allowed to make a check's funds available before the transfer is actually verified — a check can look "cleared" and spendable for days before the bank discovers it's fake and reverses it
  • Contact the brand named in the ad directly through its official website to verify whether it actually runs a car wrap advertising program
  • Only use payment apps like Venmo or Cash App to send money to people you personally know, never a "vendor" a stranger tells you to pay
  • Report suspected car wrap scams to BBB Scam Tracker and the FTC

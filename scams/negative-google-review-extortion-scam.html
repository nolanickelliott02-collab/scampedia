Negative Google Review Extortion Scam

Added Oct 20, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: Google Fraud and Scams Advisory, November 2025, ConsumerAffairs

Overview

Scammers flood a small business's Google Business Profile with fake one-star reviews, then contact the owner demanding payment, goods, or services to make the bad reviews disappear.

How It Works

The attack typically starts with "review-bombing," where fraudsters flood a business's Google listing with inauthentic one-star reviews, often using copy-and-paste language across multiple fake accounts whose profiles may lack photos, have few past reviews, or show reviews scattered across unrelated industries and locations. Once the business's rating has visibly dropped, the scammers reach out directly to the owner, frequently through third-party messaging apps like WhatsApp, and demand money to remove the negative reviews. They threaten that if the business doesn't pay, the fake reviews will stay up or the attack will escalate further, pressuring owners who fear reputational damage into paying even though victims report the cycle does not stop once they pay.

Red Flags

  • A sudden, unexplained spike in one-star reviews appearing all at once
  • Multiple negative reviews containing repeated or nearly identical copy-and-paste wording
  • Reviewer profiles with no profile photo, few past reviews, or reviews scattered across unrelated industries and locations
  • Someone contacting the business owner via WhatsApp or another third-party messaging app demanding payment to remove reviews
  • Threats that more fake reviews will be posted or the attack will escalate if payment isn't made

Real Examples

  • A Los Angeles contractor received a WhatsApp message from a number in Pakistan warning that someone had ordered 20 negative reviews of her business; her once-perfect 5-star Google rating soon plunged to 3.6, and she paid $250 in two separate payments before realizing the cycle would not stop.
  • A moving company owner in Georgia received a WhatsApp message from a man who later admitted to selling negative Google reviews for $100 per batch of 20; the fake reviews described elaborate false complaints like boxes smashed in front of customers, and new fakes kept appearing even after Google removed some.

How to Protect Yourself

  • Never pay or engage with the scammers to have reviews removed -- payment rarely stops the attacks and can invite more
  • Take and keep screenshots of the fake reviews and any related messages (emails, texts, WhatsApp) to document the harassment or extortion attempt
  • Report suspicious reviews to Google using the "report review" option directly in Google Maps, providing details explaining why it's fake
  • Report extortion attempts using Google's official merchant extortion report form
  • Consider removing your cellphone number from online listings, as this has helped stop WhatsApp harassment for at least one victim

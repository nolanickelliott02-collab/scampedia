Tech Support Pop-Up Scam

Added Apr 1, 2016· Source: FTC Consumer Alerts (general reference, not linked to a specific article)
Overview

A scary browser pop-up claims your computer is infected with viruses and displays a 'Microsoft' or 'Apple' phone number. Calling it connects you to scammers who request remote access to your computer.

How It Works

While browsing, a victim's browser is hijacked by a malicious ad or script that displays a full-screen warning designed to look like a Windows or macOS system alert, complete with fake virus scan animations, alarming sound effects, and a phone number to call for "urgent" support. The pop-up is often built to lock or freeze the browser so it can't easily be closed, pressuring the victim to call the number, where a scammer posing as Microsoft or Apple support talks them into installing remote-access software, then charges for fake "repairs" or uses the access to steal financial information.

Red Flags
  • A pop-up claims to be from Microsoft, Apple, or your ISP and lists a phone number to call.
  • The browser appears frozen or won't close normally, with a countdown timer or alarm sound.
  • You're asked to install remote-access software like AnyDesk or TeamViewer for a stranger.
  • Payment is requested via gift card, wire transfer, or a "protection plan" subscription.
Where It Spreads
Fake WebsitesPhone Calls
How to Protect Yourself
  • Never call a phone number shown in a browser pop-up.
  • Legitimate companies like Microsoft and Apple do not make unsolicited support calls.
  • Close the browser tab and restart your browser to clear the pop-up.
  • Never allow remote access to your computer from an unsolicited caller.

Fake Job Offer Scam

Added Jan 1, 2012· Source: FTC Consumer Alerts (general reference, not linked to a specific article)
Overview

A recruiter offers a remote job with little to no interview, then asks the "new hire" to pay for training materials, equipment, or a background check upfront, or to deposit a check and wire back the difference.

How It Works

A scammer posts an appealing remote job listing or messages candidates directly, offering a position with little to no real interview process. Once "hired," the victim is asked to pay upfront for training materials, equipment, or a background check, or is sent a check to deposit with instructions to use part of it to purchase equipment from a specific vendor and wire back the remainder — with the original check later bouncing and leaving the victim responsible for the full amount and out any money they wired.

Red Flags
  • A job offer arrives with minimal or no real interview.
  • A request to pay for training, equipment, or a background check upfront.
  • Instructions to deposit a check and wire back a portion of it.
  • Communication happens only through chat or personal email, never a verifiable company account.
Where It Spreads
EmailSocial MediaText Messages
How to Protect Yourself
  • Be suspicious of any job offer that skips a real interview process.
  • Never pay upfront for equipment, training, or a background check.
  • Research the company independently, including looking for a real office and other employees.
  • Never deposit a check and wire money back to an "employer."

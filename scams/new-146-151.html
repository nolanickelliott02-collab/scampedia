This week's new scams

6 new scams added to Scampedia this week. Tap one to see how it works and the red flags to watch for. Also see every documented scam.

Phone Scam Nov 22, 2026

Fake Employee Self-Service Website Payroll Diversion Scam

The FBI warns that criminals are buying fraudulent search engine ads that mimic employee self-service websites (payroll, unemployment, health savings, and retirement account portals) to steal login credentials and redirect victims' direct deposits to accounts the scammers control.
Investment Scam Nov 20, 2026

Fictitious Crypto Recovery Law Firm Scam

Fraudsters pose as lawyers from real or invented law firms, contacting cryptocurrency scam victims on social media and offer to "legally" recover their stolen funds, then steal even more money through fake taxes and fees. The FBI says this tactic combines impersonation of government entities with exploitation of victims' desperation to recover prior losses.
Shopping Scam Nov 18, 2026

Fake Mortgage "Home Warranty Renewal" Mailer Scam

Homeowners receive official-looking mail disguised as an urgent notice from their mortgage lender, warning that a "home warranty" is about to lapse and must be renewed immediately for a fee — but the mailer is actually a deceptive solicitation unaffiliated with the recipient's actual mortgage company.
Rental Scam Nov 21, 2026

AI-Manipulated Airbnb Damage Claim Scam

Dishonest short-term rental hosts are using AI-generated or AI-altered photos of "damage" to file bogus reimbursement claims against guests after checkout, demanding thousands of dollars for destruction that never happened.
Rental Scam Nov 20, 2026

AI Voice-Cloned Real Estate Agent Rental Scam

Scammers steal real estate agents' real listing videos, photos, names, and license numbers from TikTok and Instagram, then use AI-cloned versions of the agents' own voices (copied from their public videos) to con apartment hunters into paying fake "refundable" application or deposit fees.
Delivery Scam Nov 18, 2026

Counterfeit Postage Label & Stamp Scam

Scammers sell fake USPS shipping labels and stamps online at steep, flat-rate discounts, leaving buyers with seized, delayed, or undelivered packages and facing possible legal consequences.

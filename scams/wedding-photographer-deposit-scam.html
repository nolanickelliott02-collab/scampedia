Wedding Photographer Deposit Scam

Added Nov 14, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: Better Business Bureau

Overview

Couples book a wedding photographer they found on social media or a wedding vendor site, pay a deposit through a peer-to-peer app, and then the photographer cancels at the last minute, never shows up, or never delivers the photos.

How It Works

After searching online for a wedding photographer, a couple finds one they like on social media or another wedding vendor site and reaches out, signs a digital contract, and sends a deposit through a peer-to-peer payment app. As the wedding date approaches, some couples report the photographer makes contact a few days before the big day to cancel after the final payment has already been made, while in other cases the photographer simply never shows up at all. In a variation of the scheme, the scam doesn't surface until after the wedding, when the couple is left waiting indefinitely for photos that never arrive. When couples try to contact the photographer to get their money back, they are unresponsive or their contact information no longer works.

Red Flags

  • Photographer insists on booking and payment entirely through social media or text, with no phone call or in-person meeting
  • Payment is requested only via peer-to-peer payment apps (which offer little buyer protection) rather than a traceable business payment method
  • Portfolio or reviews can't be verified elsewhere online
  • Contract lacks clear cancellation terms, refund policy, or the photographer's verifiable contact information
  • Photographer cancels shortly before the event after the final payment has been made

Real Examples

  • A BBB Scam Tracker report described a bride who paid a deposit and signed a contract, only for the photographer to cancel days before the wedding or simply not show up.
  • One person reported to BBB Scam Tracker that after receiving her money, the photographer stopped contacting her and her contact information went out of service; the person later found the photographer had done the same to over 30 other brides, some of whom never received their wedding photos, and reported losing $1,700.
  • Another report described a photographer who took a deposit and full payment the week before the wedding, then canceled two days before the event and kept the entire payment, with the couple reporting a loss of $5,300.

Where It Spreads

Social MediaWedding Vendor WebsitesPeer-to-Peer Payment Apps

How to Protect Yourself

  • Ask to speak by phone or meet in person before finalizing the contract and sending any deposit
  • Research the photographer independently: look at several sources for ratings and reviews, check their BBB accreditation, and search BBB Scam Tracker for reports
  • Get references and actually call them for feedback if you can't find enough information online
  • Use a payment method other than peer-to-peer apps, such as a credit card, cash, or cashier's check, and get a written contract that spells out payment terms, cancellation/refund policy, and the photographer's contact information
  • Be wary of booking services with someone who only operates and communicates through social media

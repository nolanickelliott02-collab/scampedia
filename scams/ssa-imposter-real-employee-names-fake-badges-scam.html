SSA Imposter Scam Using Real Employee Names and Fake Badges

Added Nov 6, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· 🏛️ Government-Verified· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: SSA Office of the Inspector General

Overview

Scammers pose as Social Security Administration (SSA) or SSA Office of the Inspector General (OIG) staff using the names of real employees, fake badge photos, and fake social media profiles, then claim there is a problem with your Social Security number or benefits and demand payment that is hard to trace.

How It Works

According to an April 2026 alert from the SSA Office of the Inspector General, federal law enforcement agencies are seeing a surge in government imposter scams that misuse the names of real SSA and SSA OIG employees, along with fabricated badge images and fake social media profiles. Scammers may use a real SSA employee's name with a fake badge or credential to look legitimate, or use information from a real SSA OIG employee's social media profile to impersonate them, start contact, and build trust. They may reach out by phone, text, email, social media, or mail. They claim there is a problem with your Social Security number, benefits, account, or identity, pressure you to act immediately, and demand payment by gift cards, gold bars or other precious metals, cryptocurrency, wire transfer, cash, or other payments that are hard to track. The OIG notes this matches a broader trend of criminals sending doctored images of credentials, spoofing phone numbers, and posing as government officials online.

Red Flags

  • Someone claiming to be from SSA or SSA OIG sends you a photo of a badge or credential. No real government employee will do this.
  • You are told there is a problem with your Social Security number, benefits, account, or identity and you must act right away.
  • You are asked to pay with gift cards, gold bars, cryptocurrency, a wire transfer, or cash.
  • You are threatened with arrest or legal action.
  • Someone you met through social media says they work for SSA or its Inspector General.

Real Examples

  • The SSA OIG's April 28, 2026 alert reports scammers using the name of a real SSA employee along with a fake badge or credential to appear legitimate.
  • The same alert reports scammers using information from social media profiles to impersonate a real SSA OIG employee to start contact and build trust with potential victims.

How to Protect Yourself

  • Ignore unexpected calls, texts, emails, letters, or social media messages claiming to be from SSA or SSA OIG, even if the person gives a real employee's name or sends a badge photo.
  • Do not trust caller ID. Scammers can make a call look like it comes from a government number.
  • To check, contact SSA yourself using the contact information listed on its official website, not a number or link the person gives you.
  • Create your own my Social Security account at ssa.gov to help protect against identity thieves.
  • Report fake social media profiles to the platform, and report the scam at oig.ssa.gov/report, ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and ic3.gov.
  • If you already paid or shared information, stop all contact with the scammer, tell your bank, and file a police report.

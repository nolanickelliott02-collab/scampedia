Fake Class Action Settlement Notice Scam

Added Oct 14, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: ConsumerAffairs

Overview

Scammers send fake emails and texts claiming you're owed money from a class-action lawsuit settlement, using the promise of a payout to steal personal data, bank details, or upfront "processing fees."

How It Works

Scammers send unsolicited emails, texts, or social media messages designed to look like official class-action settlement notices, claiming the recipient is eligible for a cash payout from a lawsuit. The messages mimic real settlement administrator communications but push victims to click a link where they're asked to hand over sensitive information like Social Security numbers and bank account details, or to pay an upfront "processing" fee before their winnings can supposedly be sent. In reality, legitimate settlements never require payment to receive money owed, and genuine claim forms only need basic contact information for mailing a check or payment.

Red Flags

  • Request for a Social Security number or full bank account details to "process" a settlement payment
  • Any demand for an upfront processing fee or wiring money before funds are released
  • Missing case details such as case number, court name, law firm names, or no official settlement website
  • A notice received by email, text, or social media about a case the recipient wasn't previously aware of

Real Examples

  • ConsumerAffairs reported in October 2025 that scammers were sending emails and texts claiming recipients were eligible for class-action settlement payments, using the claims to harvest personal data or raid bank accounts

Where It Spreads

EmailText MessagesSocial Media

How to Protect Yourself

  • Independently search the case name to confirm it appears in a real court docket or known settlement website rather than clicking any link in the message
  • Visit the official settlement website directly by typing the URL yourself instead of following a link from an email or text
  • Never provide a Social Security number or bank account information to claim a settlement "” legitimate administrators only need basic contact info and rarely need such details until payment time, and even then only through secure channels
  • Remember that genuine settlements never require you to pay a fee or wire money to receive funds you're owed
  • If you suspect it's a scam, don't respond, and report it to the FTC via reportfraud.ftc.gov

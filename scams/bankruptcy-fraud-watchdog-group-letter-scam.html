"Bankruptcy Fraud Watchdog Group" Letter Scam

Added Nov 8, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· 🏛️ Government-Verified· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: U.S. Department of Justice, U.S. Trustee Program -

Overview

Scammers posing as a "Bankruptcy Fraud Watchdog Group" send letters to people going through bankruptcy, falsely accusing them of hiding assets and demanding a $450 "amnesty fine" paid in Bitcoin via QR code to avoid prosecution.

How It Works

A scam operator calling itself the "Bankruptcy Fraud Watchdog Group" targets consumer debtors who are going through bankruptcy proceedings. The group sends official-looking letters falsely accusing the debtor of failing to disclose assets in their bankruptcy case and threatens referral to "the United States Bankruptcy Trustee" for fraud and possible criminal prosecution unless the debtor promptly pays a "one-time amnesty fine" of $450. Victims are told to pay via Bitcoin, scanning a QR code included in the letter, supposedly to make the accusation go away. The letters also falsely warn debtors that if they share the letter with their own attorney, they will face "immediate referral for criminal prosecution" — a tactic designed to isolate victims from the legal help that would normally expose the scam.

Red Flags

  • Unsolicited letter from an unofficial-sounding group ("Bankruptcy Fraud Watchdog Group") rather than the actual U.S. Trustee Program or bankruptcy court
  • Demand for a cryptocurrency payment, such as Bitcoin via QR code, to resolve a legal accusation
  • Threat of criminal prosecution unless a fast "amnesty fine" is paid
  • Instruction telling you NOT to tell your attorney or warning of consequences if you share the letter
  • Use of urgent legal-sounding language and a fixed settlement amount ($450) to pressure quick payment

Real Examples

  • The U.S. Trustee Program issued a nationwide alert describing letters sent by the 'Bankruptcy Fraud Watchdog Group' threatening debtors with referral to 'the United States Bankruptcy Trustee' for fraud unless they paid a $450 'amnesty fine' in Bitcoin via QR code.

Where It Spreads

Physical Mail

How to Protect Yourself

  • Know that the real U.S. Trustee Program does not demand payment in Bitcoin or via QR code to resolve bankruptcy fraud allegations
  • Always share any letter accusing you of bankruptcy fraud with your bankruptcy attorney immediately, regardless of what the letter claims
  • Report suspicious letters to the USTP's bankruptcy fraud hotline or your local U.S. Trustee office
  • Never pay a legal fine or settlement via cryptocurrency QR code, which is untraceable and irreversible

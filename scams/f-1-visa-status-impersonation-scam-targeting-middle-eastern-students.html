F-1 Visa Status Impersonation Scam Targeting Middle Eastern Students

Added Nov 12, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· 🏛️ Government-Verified· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3), "Impersonation Scheme Targeting Middle Eastern Students in the United States," Alert Number I-051325-PSA

Overview

Scammers impersonate U.S. or foreign immigration/government officials to tell foreign students on F-1 visas they are "out of status" and must pay or share personal information immediately. The FBI says the scheme has specifically targeted students from the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Jordan.

How It Works

According to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3), scammers contact foreign students lawfully studying in the United States, or who are in the process of coming to the United States, and impersonate government or immigration officials claiming the student is out of status for violations of F-1 student visa requirements or otherwise facing immigration issues. Victims are threatened with prosecution or deportation and asked to pay an unknown entity or bank account to process immigration paperwork, pay university registration fees, or pay a legal fee. Scammers often claim to be from DHS, HSI, or USCIS to add credibility, may spoof phone numbers of government agencies, foreign embassies, or universities, and may use accents or language matching the purported location of the callers.

Red Flags

  • Unsolicited call or message claiming your student visa is suddenly out of status
  • Caller claims to be a government or immigration official and demands immediate action or payment
  • Caller sometimes claims to represent a foreign country's government instead of the U.S.
  • Pressure to act quickly to avoid supposed arrest, deportation, or legal consequences
  • Request for personal, financial, or identification details over the phone

Real Examples

  • The FBI reports the scheme has thus far targeted students from the United Arab Emirates (UAE), Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Jordan.
  • In some instances, scammers vary the scheme by posing as government officials from foreign countries, including the UAE and Qatar, rather than U.S. officials.
  • The FBI notes that in 2024, government impersonation fraud schemes of this type cost the American public over $405 million in losses from over 17,000 complaints.

How to Protect Yourself

  • Do not provide personal or financial information to unsolicited callers claiming to be immigration or government officials
  • Verify you are speaking with a government official by hanging up and contacting the office through a third-party obtained number, then asking for the agent or department you were speaking with
  • Verify that any website visited is a secure or encrypted site, as government websites are, and watch for spoofed domains like 'gov.org' mimicking '.gov'
  • If you believe you are the target of this scam, gather all relevant documentation and contact your home country's embassy, the FBI, or the State Department's Bureau of Diplomatic Security
  • Report suspected incidents to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3.gov)

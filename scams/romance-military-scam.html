Romance / Military Scam

Added Jan 1, 2010· Source: FTC Consumer Alerts (general reference, not linked to a specific article)
Overview

Fake online dating profiles — often claiming to be deployed military, offshore oil workers, or traveling doctors — build a relationship over weeks before creating a financial crisis requiring money.

How It Works

A scammer builds a fake dating or social media profile, often using photos stolen from a real service member, doctor, or engineer, and reaches out with flattering, attentive messages that quickly escalate into a whirlwind long-distance romance. Because the persona is "deployed overseas," "on an oil rig," or "on a humanitarian mission," they always have a built-in excuse for never video calling or meeting in person, and after weeks or months of relationship-building they introduce a sudden financial emergency and ask the victim to wire money or buy gift cards.

Red Flags
  • The relationship moves to declarations of love very quickly despite never meeting in person.
  • Consistent excuses to avoid video calls or in-person meetings.
  • A sudden financial emergency requiring money to be wired or sent via gift cards.
  • Profile photos are professionally lit, model-like, or reused across other profiles.
Where It Spreads
Dating AppsSocial MediaEmail
How to Protect Yourself
  • Never send money to someone you have not met in person.
  • Do a reverse image search on their profile photos — they are often stolen.
  • Be suspicious of anyone who refuses to video chat or always cancels.
  • Deployed US military personnel have access to military banking and don't need money wired.

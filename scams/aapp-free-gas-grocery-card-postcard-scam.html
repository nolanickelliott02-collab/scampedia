"AAPP" Free Gas & Grocery Card Postcard Scam

Added Nov 24, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: Better Business Bureau

Overview

Scammers mail official-looking postcards from a group called "AAPP" claiming the recipient has gas and grocery vouchers worth hundreds of dollars waiting for them, but victims who call to claim the reward are charged a "processing fee" and then hit with additional unauthorized charges.

How It Works

Consumers receive a presorted first-class postcard from an organization identified as "AAPP" stating that gas and grocery vouchers or cards (sometimes a specific dollar amount like $309.64) are being held in their name. The postcards include tracking numbers, expiration dates, and barcodes to look legitimate, and may use urgent language like "limited time offer" or "final notice" to pressure a quick response. Recipients who call the listed phone number or visit the website are told to pay a small "processing," "activation," "shipping," or "handling" fee, often around $5, using a credit or debit card to unlock the voucher. After payment information is provided, victims report additional unauthorized charges appearing on their account, being unknowingly enrolled in a monthly subscription, receiving nothing or only low-value coupons instead of the promised gift cards, and finding the company impossible to contact for a refund.

Red Flags

  • Unsolicited postcard claiming you're already approved for a 'free' gas or grocery voucher you never applied for
  • Requirement to pay a 'processing,' 'activation,' 'shipping,' or 'handling' fee before receiving a supposedly free reward
  • Urgent language on the mailer such as 'limited time offer' or 'final notice'
  • Tracking numbers, barcodes, and expiration dates used to make a scam mailer look official

Real Examples

  • A Northwest Georgia resident reported receiving a postcard from 'AAPP' claiming $309.64 in gas and grocery vouchers was being held in his name
  • A woman reported to BBB Scam Tracker that her sister received a postcard from AAPP, presorted first-class mail from a Tampa phone number, stating she was eligible for gas and grocery cards; she agreed to pay a $5 processing fee and was then immediately charged an additional $46, for a total loss of $51

How to Protect Yourself

  • Remember that legitimate prizes and rewards never require you to pay a fee first to receive them
  • Don't rely on information printed on a postcard — independently research the company name alongside words like 'BBB,' 'reviews,' or 'scam' before calling or visiting any listed website
  • Never provide payment card information under pressure to act immediately in response to an unsolicited mailer offering a free gift
  • Check your bank and credit card statements closely after any such contact and dispute unauthorized charges immediately with your financial institution
  • Report suspicious postcards to BBB Scam Tracker, your state's consumer protection office, and the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov

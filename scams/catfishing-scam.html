Romance Scam

Catfishing Scam

Added Jan 1, 2008· Source: FTC Consumer Alerts (general reference, not linked to a specific article)
Overview

Someone creates a fake online identity, often using stolen photos of an attractive stranger, to build a relationship for emotional manipulation, financial gain, or simply to deceive without necessarily asking for money.

How It Works

Someone builds a fake online persona using photos and details stolen from a real person's social media, then reaches out to targets on dating apps, social media, or messaging platforms to build a relationship under the false identity. The motive varies — some catfish for financial gain, some for emotional validation or control, and some simply to deceive without ever asking for money — but the relationship is built entirely on a fabricated identity that unravels under any real scrutiny, like a video call or an in-person meeting.

Red Flags
  • Profile photos look professionally shot or model-quality for an average dating profile.
  • Inconsistent details about their job, hometown, or daily life across conversations.
  • Constant excuses to avoid meeting in person or video chatting.
  • A reverse image search shows the photos belong to someone else entirely.
Where It Spreads
Dating AppsSocial Media
How to Protect Yourself
  • Reverse image search profile photos to check if they've been stolen from someone else.
  • Insist on a live video call early in any online relationship.
  • Be wary of inconsistent details about their job, location, or life story.
  • Trust your instincts if something about their story doesn't add up.

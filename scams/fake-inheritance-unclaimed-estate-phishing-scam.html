Fake Inheritance / Unclaimed Estate Phishing Scam

Added Oct 22, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: Fox News (CyberGuy Report), Pennsylvania Insurance Department

Overview

Scammers send polished, error-free emails claiming the recipient is a beneficiary of a large unclaimed inheritance or estate, sometimes impersonating state government employees, in order to extract upfront "release" fees or harvest personal data through phishing links.

How It Works

Victims receive an unsolicited, professional-looking email informing them they are a "Primary Potential Beneficiary" of an unclaimed inheritance or estate, often from a distant relative or tied to a fabricated organization. The message creates urgency with a short deadline, warning the funds will be forfeited or reassigned if the recipient doesn't act immediately, and includes a button or link to "claim" the money, which actually harvests personal and financial data. In a related, more elaborate version, scammers impersonate real state government employees (such as from an insurance department or banking agency) and a foreign credit union, telling an older victim a wealthy distant relative left them an inheritance, then pressuring them to wire upfront "release" fees through legitimate, licensed financial institutions over an extended period before any inheritance is ever paid out.

Red Flags

  • Unsolicited email claiming you're owed a large unclaimed inheritance you never knew about
  • Artificial urgency, such as a short countdown (e.g., 48 hours) before funds are supposedly forfeited
  • Requests to click a button or link to 'check' or 'claim' your inheritance
  • Demands for upfront fees, taxes, or 'release' payments before any inheritance is delivered
  • Use of official-sounding but non-existent registry or agency names
  • Sender impersonating a government employee, bank, or foreign credit union representative

Real Examples

  • A Fox News reader identified only as "Tim C." received a "Heir Research Registry" email claiming he was a "Primary Potential Beneficiary" of an unclaimed estate, with a 48-hour deadline and a "Check My Unclaimed Inheritance" button designed to steal his data.
  • Pennsylvania's Insurance Department, Department of Aging, Department of Banking and Securities, State Police, and Attorney General's Office jointly warned residents after an older Pennsylvanian received an email impersonating a Commonwealth employee and a foreign credit union representative, claiming a wealthy distant relative had left a large inheritance, then was pressured to pay upfront fees through licensed financial institutions totaling hundreds of thousands of dollars over nearly a year.

How to Protect Yourself

  • Never click links or buttons in unexpected inheritance or estate emails; go directly to your state's official unclaimed property website instead
  • Do not pay any fee, tax, or charge to receive an inheritance "you didn't know you had" ", legitimate estates never require upfront payment to release funds
  • Verify any claimed government agency or employee by calling the agency directly using a number from its official .gov website, not one provided in the email
  • Be skeptical of deadlines pressuring you to act within hours; real legal and probate processes do not operate on urgent email countdowns
  • Report suspicious inheritance emails to the FTC, your state Attorney General, and forward phishing emails to the appropriate reporting address

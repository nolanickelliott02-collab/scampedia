Fake Social Security Statement Email Scam

Added Oct 2, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· 🏛️ Government-Verified· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: SSA Office of the Inspector General

Overview

Scammers are mass-emailing fake notices claiming your Social Security statement is ready to download, tricking recipients into clicking malicious links or opening attachments. The SSA's Office of Inspector General warns this is a surge in government imposter phishing, separate from phone-based SSN "suspension" scams.

How It Works

Victims receive an unsolicited email designed to look like an official Social Security Administration message, complete with government-style logos, colors, and formatting, claiming their Social Security statement is ready to view or download. The email contains a link or attachment labeled as the official document; clicking it can install malware on the victim's device or redirect them to a fake website designed to harvest personal and financial information, which can then be used for identity theft or financial fraud.

Red Flags

  • Unsolicited email claiming your Social Security statement is ready to download
  • Embedded links or attachments labeled as official statements or documents
  • Sender email address does not end in '.gov'
  • Message creates urgency or pressure to act immediately
  • Requests to pay via gift cards, prepaid debit cards, wire transfer, cryptocurrency, cash, or gold bars, or threats of arrest/benefit suspension

Real Examples

  • SSA OIG issued a February 19, 2026 nationwide scam alert describing a 'significant increase in government imposter scam emails' falsely offering access to a Social Security statement
  • SSA OIG noted the fake emails use official-looking logos, colors, and formatting and that links may install malware or direct victims to fake data-harvesting websites

Where It Spreads

Email

How to Protect Yourself

  • Do not click links or open attachments in unsolicited emails claiming to be from SSA
  • Go directly to ssa.gov/myaccount to access or set up your real Social Security account rather than using any link in an email
  • Verify that any official SSA email address ends in '.gov' before trusting it
  • If you clicked a link or shared information, stop all contact with the sender, contact your financial institutions, and report the incident to SSA OIG, the FBI's IC3, and the FTC
  • Report suspicious emails immediately to the SSA Office of Inspector General

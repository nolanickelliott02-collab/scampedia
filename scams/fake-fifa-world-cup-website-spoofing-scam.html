Fake FIFA World Cup Website Spoofing Scam

Added Nov 12, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· 🏛️ Government-Verified· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: FBI/IC3 Public Service Announcement, "Threat Actors Spoofing FIFA Websites in Advance of the 2026 World Cup," I-052726-PSA

Overview

The FBI warns that cyber criminals are spoofing FIFA's official website with lookalike domains to steal personal data and sell fake 2026 World Cup tickets and hospitality packages.

How It Works

Ahead of the 2026 FIFA World Cup, threat actors are building large numbers of websites that mimic the real fifa.com site using typosquatted or lookalike domain names. The FBI/IC3 says these fake sites are used to collect victims' personal information, sell counterfeit World Cup tickets and hospitality products, and possibly facilitate other malicious activity, including fake job and HR recruitment pages. Victims searching for tickets, hospitality packages, or even FIFA job openings can land on one of these spoofed pages, enter payment or account details, and end up with stolen credentials, stolen money, or bogus tickets that won't get them into any match.

Red Flags

  • Web addresses that resemble fifa.com but contain extra words, hyphens, or altered spelling, such as fifa-ticket[.]live, wvvw-fifa[.]com, or fifa-com[.]services
  • Ticket, hospitality, job, or HR pages reached through a search ad or social media link instead of navigating directly from the official FIFA homepage
  • Pressure to buy tickets or hospitality packages quickly from a site you didn't reach by typing www.fifa.com yourself

Real Examples

  • The FBI identified spoofed ticket-themed domains including fifa-ticket[.]live and quiniela-fifa-2026.pages[.]dev
  • The FBI identified fake FIFA job/HR-themed domains including jobs-fifa[.]com, fifa-hr[.]com, fifa-careerhub[.]com, and fifa-hiring[.]com
  • The FBI identified lookalike/typo domains such as wvvw-fifa[.]com, ww-fifa[.]com, fifa-com[.]com, and www.fifa-com[.]services

Where It Spreads

WebsitesSearch Engine AdsSocial Media

How to Protect Yourself

  • Verify that the URL of the FIFA website ends in .com and is correctly entered as www.fifa.com before entering any information
  • Navigate to subdomains such as plus.fifa.com directly from the official FIFA homepage rather than clicking links from ads, emails, or social media
  • Treat any FIFA-branded job posting, ticket sale, or hospitality offer found through a search engine or social ad with suspicion and confirm it on FIFA's own site first
  • Report suspected spoofed FIFA sites or losses to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center at ic3.gov

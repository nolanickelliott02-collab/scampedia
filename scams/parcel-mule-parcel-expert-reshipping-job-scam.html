Parcel Mule / "Parcel Expert" Reshipping Job Scam

Added Oct 29, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: Malwarebytes

Overview

Scammers recruit people via unsolicited messages for a fake work-from-home "Parcel Expert" job that actually involves receiving, repackaging, and reshipping merchandise bought with stolen credit cards, turning victims into unwitting mules for stolen goods.

How It Works

Targets are contacted out of the blue by email, social media DM, or WhatsApp with a job offer from someone claiming to work in a "Logistics Department" for a well-known retailer or shipping company. The message offers a flexible, high-paying remote role — titled something like "Parcel Expert" — where the victim's only duties are to receive packages at their home address, check the items against invoices, take photos, repackage them, and forward them to a "distribution hub," sometimes internationally. In reality, the packages contain merchandise purchased with stolen payment card information, and the victim is unknowingly helping criminals launder stolen goods by breaking the shipping trail. Victims are often asked to hand over personal documents and banking details during a fake "onboarding" process, and are never actually paid for the "work."

Red Flags

  • Unsolicited job offer for well-paid remote work received, inspecting, and reshipping packages
  • Legitimate companies do not ship high-value goods to a private home address of someone they just hired online
  • Job offer invokes trusted big-name retailers or carriers (Amazon, FedEx, Walmart, Best Buy, DHL, AliExpress, Zappos) as a trust signal
  • High pay for low-skill, home-based work with flexible hours and no interview
  • Requests to send packages onward to another address, hub, or international destination
  • Requests for personal ID documents or banking details during onboarding

Real Examples

  • Malwarebytes documented a message reading in part: "My name is Elena from the Logistics Department. We reviewed your profile and would like to offer you a position as a Parcel Expert" promising a remote part-time role paying up to $5,300 a month for receiving, checking, and forwarding packages.
  • Malwarebytes found the companies named in these offers vary by region: a German target was recruited to work for DHL, a Spanish target received an offer for 'mercado,' and a US target got an offer referencing a company called Freight Metro, with Amazon, FedEx, Best Buy, AliExpress, and Walmart also commonly invoked.

How to Protect Yourself

  • Never accept a job that requires you to receive and reship packages from your home address, especially if contacted out of the blue
  • Research the hiring company independently and search for complaints or scam reports rather than relying on links sent by a recruiter
  • Never give copies of your ID, banking information, or other sensitive documents to an online 'employer' before any formal, verifiable hiring process
  • Be suspicious of any job that promises high income for minimal, vague logistics work with flexible hours
  • Do not interact with the scammer, do not forward any packages you did not personally order, and report the incident to the proper authorities (in the US, the FTC)

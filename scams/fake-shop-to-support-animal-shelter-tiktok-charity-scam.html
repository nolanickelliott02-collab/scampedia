Fake "Shop to Support" Animal Shelter TikTok Charity Scam

Added Oct 10, 2026· 🤖 AI-researched· Editor: Nick Elliott· Source: Better Business Bureau (BBB Scam Tracker / BBB of Eastern Carolinas) -

Overview

Scammers post heartstring-tugging TikTok and social media videos of "elderly" shelter owners making handmade pet products to save a failing animal shelter, then pocket the money when shoppers buy the never-delivered merchandise.

How It Works

While scrolling TikTok or another social platform, a user sees a video of an older adult asking for help, often showing cats or other animals in a shelter along with captions claiming the person is struggling to keep their shelter open and is making products like cat toys or slippers to fund it. The video shows craft-store footage and a finished product, uses emotional music and urgent captions, and includes a link in the description directing viewers to a website to buy the item "to support the cause." Shoppers who order the merchandise never receive anything, and it is unclear whether any money actually goes to a real charity; BBB has found the accompanying videos, photos, and backstories are frequently AI-generated or stolen from other accounts rather than depicting a real shelter or person.

Red Flags

  • Videos use urgent emotional pleas like "please stay for 8 seconds to help my cat shelter from shutting down" or "please don't scroll for 12 seconds to help my failing animal shelter"
  • The featured charity or shelter has no verifiable information, address, or nonprofit status anywhere online
  • Heavy use of touching music, cute animals, and elderly people designed to trigger sympathy and impulse purchases
  • A purchase link leads to a standalone sales website rather than a verified charity donation page
  • Videos and product stories appear reused across multiple accounts selling different items (cat toys, cow slippers, etc.)

Real Examples

  • BBB Scam Tracker received a report describing a TikTok account called FlappyBirdUS showing "a little old man and his wife who 'make cat toys to support their failing cat shelter'" that led to a site called meowwings.com where buyers paid but received nothing and could find no real information about the shelter, with the same fake, AI-generated videos recycled under other accounts selling cow slippers instead.

How to Protect Yourself

  • Don't give in to videos that tug at your heartstrings or create a sense of urgency, even if you're excited about the product
  • Check to see if the charity is real on Give.org before purchasing or donating
  • Look up the company name, website, and contact information, read reviews on bbb.org, and search the company's name with the word "scam" before buying
  • Only do business with companies you know and trust, or research unfamiliar companies thoroughly first
  • Give locally by contacting your local or state animal shelters or other agencies directly to ask how you can support them
